Labour Cost
📋 Labour Days — Based on CPWD DSR Productivity Standards + Site Reality

Days are pre-calculated using CPWD Schedule of Rates (DSR) productivity constants — the official Government of India standard for construction labour output per day. This gives a scientifically grounded starting point rather than a guess.

However, actual site days may differ due to: curing wait times between concrete pours (7–28 days per IS 456), monsoon shutdowns, festival breaks, sand/aggregate bans, local political situations, and hill site access delays. Review and adjust the pre-filled days based on your site knowledge. Increasing workers automatically reduces calendar days.

Days are auto-calculated using CPWD DSR productivity standards:
• Bar Bender — 600 kg steel per worker per day (CPWD Schedule of Rates)
• Shuttering Carpenter — 100 sqft formwork per worker per day
• Concreting Mason — 2.5 m³ concrete per mason per day
• Curing — IS 456 minimum 14 days per floor curing cycle
• Site staff (Engineer, Foreman, Watchman) — full project duration

You can override any value. Adjust workers (+ / − buttons) to change calendar days automatically. Edit daily rate to match your local area. Click — to remove any trade not needed. Add custom trades at the bottom.

Technical Quality Reminders — IS Code Based
Based on IS 456:2000, IS 13920:2016, IS 1199, IS 516:1959. These are completely free to read.

💧 Concrete Curing (IS 456 Cl.13.5)

  • Start curing within 24 hours of placing
  • Minimum 7 days for OPC concrete
  • Recommended 28 days for full strength
  • Keep surfaces continuously wet
  • Never allow premature drying in summer

🧪 Slump Test (IS 1199:2018)

  • Test before EVERY concrete batch
  • Columns: 25–75mm target slump
  • Slabs: 50–100mm target slump
  • Collapse slump = reject the batch
  • Never add water on site to increase slump

🧊 Cube Testing (IS 516:1959)

  • 3 cubes minimum per 50m³ concrete
  • Test at 7 days — ≥70% of 28-day strength
  • Final test at 28 days — IS 456 criterion
  • Cure cubes in water tank only
  • Alert engineer if 7-day is below 65%

📏 Cover to Steel (IS 456 Table 16)

  • Footings: 50mm clear cover always
  • Columns: 40mm clear cover
  • Beams: 25–30mm clear cover
  • Slabs: 20mm minimum cover
  • Use concrete blocks — never brick pieces

🔩 Reinforcement (IS 13920)

  • Never weld TMT bars
  • Lap length: minimum 45 × bar diameter
  • Zone III and above: Fe500D mandatory
  • Bar bending done cold — never heat
  • Stagger laps — never at same section

🏗️ Formwork Removal (IS 456 Table 4)

  • Columns: minimum 24 hours
  • Slab props: minimum 7 days
  • Beam props: 14–21 days by span
  • Use needle vibrator every pour
  • Max concrete free fall: 1.5m only

⚗️ Water-Cement Ratio (IS 456 Table 5)

  • M20: maximum W/C ratio 0.55
  • M25: maximum W/C ratio 0.50
  • M30: maximum W/C ratio 0.45
  • Potable water only — IS 456 Cl. 5.4
  • Use admixture for workability not water

🪖 Safety (IS 4912)

  • Helmet and shoes — ALL workers always
  • Full body harness required G+2 and above
  • Safety net mandatory G+3 and above
  • Inspect scaffolding every week
  • No work on scaffolding during rain

What This Report Does NOT Include

The structural frame is just the skeleton of your building — the columns, beams, slabs and foundations. A complete liveable house needs much more. Budget for these separately.

⚠️

Important: The Grand Total in this report covers the bare RCC structural frame only. For a complete house, you will typically need to add 60% to 100% more for finishing work. A structural frame costing ₹40 lakhs usually becomes a complete home costing ₹65 to ₹80 lakhs at standard finish. Do not approach your bank or plan your budget based on the structural frame cost alone.

⛔ Engineering & Legal Disclaimer — Please Read Carefully

Nature of Calculations: Material quantities in this report are derived from empirical industry thumb rules and standard Indian construction productivity constants (CPWD DSR), not from a custom structural analysis of your specific building. The material grades (M25, Fe500D, etc.) and IS code compliance checks follow IS 456:2000, IS 1893:2016, IS 13920:2016, and IS 1786:2008. However, the quantity calculations are estimates based on standard construction ratios, not engineered design.

What This Report Is: A preliminary budgeting tool. Suitable for cost planning before engaging a structural engineer. Not suitable for procurement, tendering, or construction execution.

Seismic Zone Limitation: Seismic zones are auto-assigned at state level. Large states (Maharashtra, UP, Rajasthan) span multiple seismic zones. For accurate zone classification, verify your city-level zone from IS 1893:2016 Annex E with your structural engineer.

Mandatory Before Construction: (1) Geotechnical soil investigation per IS 1888, (2) Detailed structural design by a licensed Structural Engineer per IS 456:2000 + IS 1893:2016, (3) Approved drawings from municipal authority, (4) Structural engineer certification.
